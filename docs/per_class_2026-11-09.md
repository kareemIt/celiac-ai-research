# Experiment 1: per-class error structure, from saved predictions (run 2026-11-09)

> **Corrected later the same day — read [`validation_2026-11-09.md`](validation_2026-11-09.md) §1
> before quoting anything below.** Every number here averages per *fold*. The reference arm
> (`scaffold_pub`) pools up to 113 publications per fold while the shifted arm holds exactly one, so
> the two are not aggregated on the same unit. On the corrected publication unit the asymmetry keeps
> its direction but loses its significance (TG2 sensitivity: 10/10 → 8/10 models, sign-test
> p 0.0020 → 0.109, Holm-significant 2 → 0), and the cross-target picture becomes two targets
> leaning sensitivity and one leaning specificity rather than three distinct signatures. This
> document is kept unaltered as the record of what was run first.

Reproduce: `python scripts/analyse_per_class.py`.
Outputs: `results/per_class_{tg2,cdk2,maob}.csv` (one row per model × arm × fold × operating point)
and `results/per_class_*_summary.csv`. Tests: `tests/test_per_class.py`.
Cost: about 40 seconds of CPU, no GPU, no retraining, no new data.
Plan and pre-registration: [`review_2026-11-09.md`](review_2026-11-09.md) §5.

**The question.** `src/evaluation/metrics.py:classification` reported AUROC, AUPRC, MCC, ECE, Brier
and log loss, and no file in `results/` carried sensitivity, specificity, accuracy or a confusion
matrix. So the project could not say whether its models fail on actives or on inactives — the
supervisor's objection. This computes that from predictions already committed.

**The answer, in one line.** The publication-shift penalty has a **class signature, it is
target-specific, and AUROC cannot see it**: on TG2 the penalty falls almost entirely on
sensitivity, on MAO-B almost entirely on specificity, and on CDK2 on both about equally.

---

## 1. What was computed, and the one deviation from plan

Three operating points, fixed before any output was read:

| | definition | uses test labels? |
|---|---|---|
| `t050` | the 0.5 cut the project already reports through MCC | no |
| `prior` | the training fold's own prevalence, from `results/splits_<target>.csv` | no |
| `oracle` | the cut maximising balanced accuracy on the test fold itself | **yes** |

**Deviation, recorded.** The plan's second point was a balanced-accuracy cut *fitted on the training
fold*. Training-fold predictions were never saved, so that cut cannot be computed without a retrain,
which this experiment was specifically designed to avoid. `oracle` replaces it and brackets it from
above: no train-fitted rule can beat a cut chosen with the test labels in hand. §4 shows the oracle
is not usable as a performance estimate.

**Verification gate.** The script recomputes AUROC and MCC at 0.5 from the predictions and refuses
to write anything unless they match the committed `sota_*.csv` tables. They match to within 1e-9
across **510 TG2 folds, 369 CDK2 folds and 170 MAO-B folds**, so the join is reading the rows it
thinks it is.

Comparisons use `scaffold_pub` against the shifted arm, because those arms cover the same molecules
— the splitting rule is then the only variable. Welch's unpaired t-test, matching the existing
convention for unpaired arms, with Holm applied across models within each rate.

---

## 2. The main result: the penalty is asymmetric, and the asymmetry differs by target

TG2, `scaffold_pub` (20 folds, median 44 molecules) → `paper_lopo` (11 folds, median 38), at the
0.5 cut everything in the project currently uses:

| model | sensitivity | specificity | predicted-positive rate | AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Random forest | 0.823 → **0.320** | 0.679 → 0.761 | 0.622 → 0.303 | 0.854 → 0.612 |
| XGBoost | 0.848 → **0.347** | 0.691 → 0.816 | 0.623 → 0.274 | 0.855 → 0.573 |
| Logistic regression | 0.822 → **0.356** | 0.715 → 0.740 | 0.606 → 0.345 | 0.863 → 0.624 |
| Tabular foundation model | 0.826 → **0.435** | 0.717 → 0.770 | 0.603 → 0.343 | 0.854 → 0.653 |
| ChemBERTa-2 frozen | 0.777 → 0.527 | 0.708 → 0.649 | 0.581 → 0.457 | 0.808 → 0.701 |
| Chemprop | 0.796 → 0.796 | 0.479 → **0.329** | 0.693 → 0.689 | 0.705 → 0.542 |

Across all ten TG2 entrants:

| rate | direction | sign test | Holm-significant |
|---|---|---|---|
| sensitivity | **10 of 10 fall** | p = 0.0020 | 2 of 10 (XGBoost 0.013, random forest 0.041; logistic regression 0.051, MoLFormer and the tabular model 0.056) |
| specificity | 3 of 10 fall, 7 rise | p = 0.34 | **0 of 10** |

The random forest loses **0.503 sensitivity** and *gains* 0.081 specificity. Under publication shift
it stops predicting "potent" — its predicted-positive rate halves, from 0.622 to 0.303, on a test
set that is 54% positive. The AUROC drop from 0.854 to 0.612 reports the net of these two and says
nothing about which side moved.

**This does not generalise to the other two targets, and that is the honest headline.**

| target | shifted arm | sensitivity | specificity |
|---|---|---|---|
| TG2 | `paper_lopo` | **10/10 fall, p = 0.0020** | 3/10 fall, p = 0.34 |
| CDK2 | `paper_grouped` | 6/6 fall, p = 0.031 | 6/6 fall, p = 0.031 |
| MAO-B | `paper_grouped` | 4/6 fall, p = 0.69 | **6/6 fall, p = 0.031** |

Three targets, three different class signatures: TG2 loses actives, MAO-B loses inactives, CDK2
loses both symmetrically. Nothing survives Holm on CDK2 or MAO-B, so those are direction-consistency
results only. This is a **fourth** instance of the pattern the project already documents — findings
that look like laws on one target and do not replicate — and it should be reported as a bound, not
as a mechanism.

**Two caveats that limit the comparison.** The shifted arm is not the same protocol across targets:
TG2 uses leave-one-publication-out (11 folds of ~38), CDK2 and MAO-B use grouped publication folds
(10 folds of 210 and 512). And the three targets have different prevalence (TG2 54% positive in the
shifted arm, CDK2 56%, MAO-B 38%), which moves where the 0.5 cut sits independently of any shift.
The target-specific signature is therefore confounded with both protocol and prevalence, and
separating them needs CDK2 and MAO-B run through leave-one-publication-out at matched prevalence.

## 3. Accuracy is the wrong metric here, with a concrete example

Chemprop on TG2 `paper_lopo` posts the **highest accuracy of any model in that arm, 0.704**, with
AUROC 0.542 and balanced accuracy 0.515. It is at chance and it looks like the best model in the
arm, because it predicts "potent" for 69% of molecules in a fold that is 54% positive.

This bears directly on reading the published TG2 study, whose headline is "over 90% accuracy against
a 64% baseline". Accuracy on these folds is driven by prevalence and by where the threshold happens
to fall, not by discrimination. Our models' accuracy is now computed and committed, so the
comparison on their own metric is possible — but the right reading is that **neither side's accuracy
number should carry much weight**, and balanced accuracy with the confusion matrix beside it should
be quoted instead. The project's existing comparison against that study on AUROC under matched
splits remains the defensible one.

## 4. The 0.5 cut is misplaced, but a better cut does not repair the collapse

On the imbalanced target the 0.5 cut is simply wrong. MAO-B random forest, `paper_grouped`: at 0.5
it reaches sensitivity 0.518 and specificity 0.859 — it misses nearly half the actives. At the
prevalence-matched cut that becomes sensitivity 0.728 and specificity 0.704, with balanced accuracy
up from 0.688 to 0.716. Free, and no retraining.

On TG2 under shift, prevalence matching does almost nothing: the random forest goes from sensitivity
0.320 to 0.354, and balanced accuracy from 0.506 to 0.545. **The sensitivity collapse is in the
ranking, not in the threshold.**

The oracle cut appears to repair it — TG2 random forest balanced accuracy 0.506 → 0.711, CDK2
0.557 → 0.736, MAO-B 0.551 → 0.734. **It does not, and the pattern of gains shows why.** Mean
balanced-accuracy gain from the oracle cut, against the median size of the folds it was chosen on:

| arm | median fold | oracle gain |
|---|---|---|
| CDK2 `scaffold_pub` | 205 | +0.020 |
| MAO-B `scaffold_pub` | 507 | +0.033 |
| MAO-B `paper_grouped` | 512 | +0.042 |
| CDK2 `paper_grouped` | 210 | +0.046 |
| TG2 `scaffold` | 63 | +0.059 |
| TG2 `scaffold_pub` | 44 | +0.080 |
| CDK2 `paper_lopo` | 19 | +0.166 |
| TG2 `paper_lopo` | 38 | +0.176 |
| MAO-B `paper_lopo` | 19 | +0.183 |

The gain tracks fold size, not shift: it is three to eight times larger on folds of 19–38 molecules
than on folds of 200–500, and it is large in `paper_lopo` on all three targets regardless of each
target's class signature. Choosing one free parameter to maximise a score on 19 points recovers
about 0.17 of balanced accuracy from noise alone. **The oracle numbers are an upper bound inflated
by selection, and must not be read as achievable performance.** They are reported because leaving
them out would hide the one analysis that could have overturned §2.

## 5. What this adds, and what it does not

**Adds.** A fifth empirical item of the kind the project already contributes: a reported
publication-shift penalty in AUROC has a class signature underneath it that AUROC cannot express,
and on our three targets that signature is not the same twice. Anyone quoting a single
threshold-free number for this penalty is averaging over the question of *which* molecules the model
now gets wrong — which, for a screening cascade meant to prioritise candidates for assay, is the
question that matters. Missing 68% of actives and missing 30% of inactives are not the same failure
and have the same AUROC.

**Does not add.** No new model, no accuracy improvement, and no claim about TG2 inhibition or celiac
disease. The asymmetry is an observation about evaluation, measured on predictions that were already
committed, and it is one target at Holm significance and two at direction consistency only.

**Not done.** The per-publication aggregation unit is not applied here — the summary averages folds,
matching the existing `sota_*_summary.csv` convention, so the §5.14 aggregation-unit correction has
not been redone for these rates. Cluster-bootstrap intervals over publications are not computed. The
confounding named at the end of §2 is unresolved.

**If this enters the paper**, it needs the CDK2/MAO-B leave-one-publication-out runs at matched
protocol first, since as it stands the cross-target comparison changes two things at once.
