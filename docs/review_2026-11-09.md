The brief asks for eight deliverables. Six are already in the repository, in some cases in more
detail than the brief asks for. Three genuine gaps remain, and one of them blocks a direct

the gaps with evidence, and proposes three experiments.

## 2. The two named papers, verified

**Paper 1 — confirmed, and already reference [1].** Wichka I, Lai P-K. *Rapid discovery of
Transglutaminase 2 inhibitors for celiac disease with boosting ensemble machine learning.*
Computational and Structural Biotechnology Journal 2024;23:3669–3679,
doi:10.1016/j.csbj.2024.10.019. Stevens Institute of Technology. Its data is already in
`data/raw/tg2/` and its baselines reproduce exactly (§3.1). Reported headline: boosted ensembles of
perceptron learners and low-depth random forests above **90% accuracy** against a **64%** baseline;
a terminal Michael acceptor and a sulfonamide group flagged as important; a `Celiac Informatics`
web app released. Note the brief quotes "93% accuracy" — the abstract says "over 90%", so the 93%
figure needs a page citation from the full text before it is repeated.

**Paper 2 — confirmed, and new to this project.** Zhang Y, et al. (ten authors, incl. Heng Ji,
Preslav Nakov). *Atomic Reasoning for Scientific Table Claim Verification.* arXiv:2506.06972, June
2025. Decomposes table-based claim checking into reusable "atomic skills" with a skill-chaining
schema; introduces the `SciAtomicBench` benchmark; reports that 350 fine-tuning examples beat
GPT-4o chain-of-thought. **It is a claim-verification paper, not a molecular-prediction paper**, and
the brief is right to insist the two be treated as separate research components.

Its relevance here is narrow but real, and not where the brief points it. `scripts/check_paper.py`
is already an atomic claim verifier — it decomposes each manuscript claim into (section, value,
source table) and recomputes it. It is deterministic and has caught a stale table, two rounding
errors, and, once section anchoring was added, correction #1 in the 2026-11-05 audit. An LLM
atomic-reasoning layer would extend that from *our own result tables* to *the source publications*,
which is the step currently done by hand. That is a tooling improvement to data curation, with no
expected effect on prediction accuracy, and it should be evaluated on a manually verified sample as
the brief says.

## 3. The negative-results audit: answers, and three gaps

Taking the brief's six audit questions in order, with current numbers.

1. **How many active and inactive compounds.** TG2: **631 molecules, 302 potent / 329 not potent**
   at a 1 µM IC50 cut (`results/splits_tg2.csv`, scaffold seed 0, all folds). Replication targets:
   CDK2 2,646 molecules across 351 publications, MAO-B 5,840 across 414.
2. **Class imbalance.** Essentially none overall — 47.9% positive. **But the folds are another
   matter**, and this is new (§3.2).
3. **Reliable inactives or ambiguous labels.** Handled carefully and documented. All 46 `Inactive`
   rows in the source carry a `>` qualifier and were being silently discarded by an earlier filter;
   they are now admitted as confirmed negatives **only when the bound sits at or above the 1 µM
   threshold**, and ignored otherwise. 45 `Inconclusive` rows are excluded on the source's own
   judgement, 30 ChEMBL rows on ChEMBL's own validity flag, and molecules "assayed" at 300 µM in the
   published classification set are not used at all. Nothing untested is treated as inactive.
4. **Molecules in both training and testing.** Measured, per split kind. Scaffold split: 0 shared
   Murcko scaffolds, test molecules with an *identical* Morgan fingerprint to a training molecule
   0.3% overall (0.0% on nine of ten seeds, 3.3% on seed 9). Random split: 28.6 shared scaffolds and
   **38.6% identical fingerprints** — which is why random-split AUROC reaches 0.95 against 0.87, and
   why random-split numbers are not reported as generalization. One caveat worth stating to the
   supervisor: the scaffold split **does not** control publication — 69.2 publications are shared
   between train and test on CDK2, 122.2 on MAO-B (`results/second_target_*_leakage.csv`).
5. **Genuine molecular relationships or memorized structures.** Measured, and the answer is
   uncomfortable. Below ~0.4 Tanimoto to the nearest training molecule every model ranks at chance
   on TG2 (`generalization.md`); that floor is dataset-specific and does not appear on CDK2 or
   MAO-B. Holding molecules fixed and changing only the split from scaffold to publication-held-out
   costs every model 0.10–0.25 AUROC, and the tuned random forest most of all (−0.242, landing at
   0.612 ± 0.178, interval including 0.5).
6. **Different performance on positives and negatives.** **Not measured. This is gap 1.**

### 3.1 Gap 1 — no threshold metrics exist anywhere in the project

`src/evaluation/metrics.py:classification` returns AUROC, AUPRC, MCC, ECE, Brier and log loss.
Across all 170+ files in `results/` there is no column for accuracy, precision, recall,
sensitivity, specificity, false-positive rate, false-negative rate, balanced accuracy or F1, and no
confusion matrix. MCC at a fixed 0.5 cut is the only threshold-dependent number reported.

Three consequences, which together make this the highest-value gap:

- The brief's question 6 — does the model fail differently on actives and inactives — **cannot be
  answered from what is committed.** That is precisely the supervisor's objection.
- The published TG2 study's headline is **accuracy**. We cannot put our models beside it on its own
  metric, so deliverable 5 is incomplete through a missing metric rather than a missing experiment.
- A 0.5 threshold is almost certainly the wrong operating point under publication shift, where mean
  predicted probability drifts per fold. Reporting sensitivity and specificity at a *calibrated*
  threshold is a different, and more honest, claim than MCC at 0.5.

The repair is cheap. `results/sota_preds*.csv` hold per-molecule `y` and `p` for every model × split
× seed (24,991 TG2 rows, 30,661 CDK2, plus MAO-B). **Every one of these metrics can be computed from
saved predictions with no retraining and no GPU.**

### 3.2 Gap 2 — validation-fold class balance swings wildly, and nobody looked

Computed here for the first time, from `results/splits_tg2.csv`, scaffold split, per seed:

| seed | 0 | 15 | 18 | 1 | 19 | median seed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| validation negatives | 54 | 48 | 42 | 17 | 23 | ~30 |
| validation positives | **5** | 15 | 16 | 46 | 42 | ~32 |
| positive fraction | **8.5%** | 23.8% | 27.6% | 73.0% | 64.6% | ~50% |

The overall dataset is balanced; individual scaffold validation folds are not, and seed 0's is 91.5%
negative. Models that use the validation fold for early stopping or threshold selection — Chemprop,
CheMeleon, ChemBERTa-2, the QLoRA runs — are being stopped on an almost single-class signal on some
seeds. The fingerprint models that do not use it (random forest, logistic regression, XGBoost) are
unaffected.

This is a **hypothesis, not a finding**: it predicts that per-seed variance should be larger for
early-stopping models than for fingerprint models, and `sota_comparison.md` §2 does show exactly
that pattern (Chemprop ± 0.123 against random forest ± 0.077, and Chemprop losing 0.096 between
seed halves against the random forest's 0.069). But the correlation has not been tested and the
direction could run the other way — deep models may simply be noisier. It is testable from saved
per-fold results plus the fold composition, again with no retraining.

### 3.3 Gap 3 — activity cliffs are cited but never measured

MoleculeACE (van Tilborg, Alenicheva & Grisoni, *JCIM* 2022;62(23):5938–5951,
doi:10.1021/acs.jcim.2c01073) is reviewed in `state_of_the_art_comparison.md` §1.0 and described
there as "the most relevant paper to our situation" — our task is a MoleculeACE-shaped task. No
cliff analysis has been run on our data. The similarity-band analysis in `generalization.md` is
adjacent but not the same measurement: it bins by *distance to training*, whereas a cliff is a pair
of *mutually similar* molecules with divergent potency. A model can score well on a band and still
invert every cliff pair inside it.

## 4. What does not need redoing

Stated plainly so effort is not spent twice.

- **Reproduction of the published TG2 study is complete and exact.** Random forest
  0.8983 ± 0.0565 against the committed 0.898 ± 0.057, maximum per-seed difference 0 across six
  metrics; logistic regression exact; XGBoost to 3.8e-8. Pinned in
  `tests/test_sota_reproduction.py`. Extending from the published 10 seeds to 20 moved the random
  forest to 0.864 ± 0.077 — the published ± 0.057 understates the true spread, and seeds 10–19 are
  harder for every model. The ranking is unchanged.
- **Boosting does not beat our incumbent.** XGBoost on Morgan fingerprints is statistically
  indistinguishable from the tuned random forest (ΔAUROC −0.010, Holm p = 0.202, within the
  pre-registered 0.05 margin). The published study's gain over its own 64% baseline is not evidence
  about our models; we never reproduced that baseline and the task definitions differ (their 0.6 µM
  active cut plus 45 inactives plus unassayed molecules at 300 µM, against our single 1 µM cut on
  measured values only).
- **Chemprop, graph neural networks, pretrained molecular models and ensembling have been run.**
  Ten entrants, three splitting protocols, 510 model fits, 6 h 30 m on one RTX 5070 Ti. Every
  learned-representation model is below the random forest, six of seven by more than the
  equivalence margin with the CI excluding zero. Brief item 5's model wish-list is largely spent;
  re-running it is not an experiment.
- **Uncertainty-aware prediction and abstention are done, including the hard version.**
  Hierarchical conformal prediction (Lee, Barber & Willett, arXiv:2306.06342) held its nominal floor
  in 54 of 54 runs against our method's 22 of 54, with three practical limits recorded — most
  importantly that on TG2 it commits to a single label on only 7.7% of molecules.
- **Selectivity against FXIII-A, ZED1227 as reference, and intestinal-delivery properties** are all
  analysed, with one claim withdrawn: ADME *is* present in the data (ChEMBL files it under the
  molecule, not the target), and our two most selective compounds are highly permeable — the wrong
  direction for a gut-restricted celiac drug. 37 molecules across 9 papers, too thin to model.

## 5. The next three experiments

Ordered by expected value per unit of compute. All three run on saved predictions; none needs a GPU
or a retrain, which is deliberate — the gaps are analysis gaps, not modelling gaps.

### Experiment 1 — Per-class error structure on saved predictions — **RUN, see [`per_class_2026-11-09.md`](per_class_2026-11-09.md)**

Outcome: the asymmetry predicted below is there on TG2 (sensitivity 10/10 models down, specificity
0/10 significant) but the *class signature differs by target* — MAO-B loses specificity instead and
CDK2 loses both. One deviation from the plan below: the train-fitted threshold needed training-fold
predictions, which were never saved, so an oracle cut replaces it as an upper bound.

**Question.** Does each model fail differently on actives and inactives, and how does that change
under publication shift?

**Method.** Extend `src/evaluation/metrics.py:classification` with sensitivity, specificity, PPV,
NPV, FPR, FNR, balanced accuracy, F1 and accuracy, plus a confusion matrix, and compute them over
`results/sota_preds*.csv` for every model × split arm × seed. Report at three operating points, all
fixed in advance: the 0.5 cut used today; the cut that maximises balanced accuracy on the
*training* fold only; and the cut matching the training prevalence. Aggregate per fold and report
cluster-bootstrapped intervals over publications in the publication arms, matching the existing
convention in `scripts/analyse_protocol_gap.py`.

**Cost.** Minutes of CPU. No new data, no retraining, no GPU.

**Expected benefit.** Answers the supervisor's central objection with committed evidence; supplies
the accuracy column needed to sit beside the published study's headline; and almost certainly shows
that the publication-arm collapse is asymmetric — if mean predicted probability drifts per fold,
specificity and sensitivity should move in opposite directions while AUROC records only the net.
That asymmetry, if present, is a new and reportable observation about the protocol gap.

**Validation criteria.** AUROC and MCC recomputed from the same predictions must match the
committed values exactly, or the loader is wrong. Pre-register the three thresholds before looking
at any output. If the per-class gap is under 0.05 in balanced accuracy across all arms, record that
as a negative result and do not promote it.

**Risk.** Low, and the main one is temptation: a new metric family invites selecting the threshold
that flatters a model. The pre-registered operating points exist to prevent that.

### Experiment 2 — Activity cliffs on all three targets — **RUN, see [`cliffs_2026-11-09.md`](cliffs_2026-11-09.md)**

Outcome: cliffs are harder in 57 of 58 model × arm comparisons (18 Holm-significant), against a
matched-potency-gap control the original design lacks. The model ranking does *not* invert
within-distribution — overall and cliff rankings correlate at ρ 0.76–0.89 in the scaffold arms —
but the correlation collapses under publication shift. One deviation: MoleculeACE's SMILES
Levenshtein test is omitted, which can only shrink the cliff set.

**Question.** Do our models invert activity cliffs, and does the ranking of models change when
scored on cliff pairs only?

**Method.** Follow the MoleculeACE definition rather than inventing one: pairs above a similarity
threshold (their paper uses substructure, scaffold and descriptor similarity) with a potency
difference above 10×. Build the pair sets for TG2, CDK2 and MAO-B, then score the *existing* saved
predictions on cliff pairs against non-cliff pairs — pairwise ranking accuracy, which is the natural
metric for a pair and avoids the threshold question entirely. Compare the model ranking on cliffs
against the headline ranking.

**Cost.** Pair enumeration is O(n²) on 631/2,646/5,840 molecules — seconds to a few minutes with
RDKit fingerprints. Scoring is a table join.

**Expected benefit.** Closes gap 3 against the paper the project itself calls most relevant. Two
outcomes are both publishable: if the model ranking inverts on cliffs, that is a direct replication
of van Tilborg et al. on a new target set and strengthens the project's central claim that the
evaluation protocol decides the conclusion. If it does not invert, that is a negative result against
a widely cited paper and worth stating.

**Validation criteria.** Cliff pairs must be enumerable deterministically from committed data with a
seed-free procedure, and the count must be reported alongside the metric — a ranking difference over
20 pairs is noise. Require at least 100 cliff pairs per target before claiming anything, and report
the pair count when the bar is not met. Intervals must bootstrap over *molecules*, not pairs, since
one molecule appears in many pairs.

**Risk.** Medium. The cliff definition has free parameters and a sweep over them is a garden of
forking paths. Fix the thresholds to MoleculeACE's published values first, report that as the
primary analysis, and treat any sensitivity sweep as explicitly secondary.

### Experiment 3 — Does fold class balance explain deep-model variance? — **RUN, negative: [`fold_balance_2026-11-09.md`](fold_balance_2026-11-09.md)**

Outcome: **no.** Every CI spans zero on both TG2 arms, r² ≤ 0.051, and the point estimates mostly
have the opposite sign to the prediction. The control-group design below had to be rewritten first:
*every* entrant tunes on the validation fold, so the control is defined by what the fold decides.
Weaker models simply vary more (mean AUROC against SD, r = −0.91 on TG2 `scaffold`), which explains
the observation that prompted the experiment. Hypothesis withdrawn; a confound is cleared from the
existing ten-entrant comparison.

**Question.** Is the larger per-seed variance of early-stopping models explained by the class
balance of the validation fold they stopped on?

**Method.** For each scaffold seed, join validation-fold positive fraction (from
`results/splits_tg2.csv`) to per-seed test AUROC (from `results/sota_tg2.csv`) for every entrant.
Regress per-seed AUROC on |validation positive fraction − 0.5|, separately for the models that
consume the validation fold and those that do not. The second group is the control: a relationship
there means something other than early stopping is driving it. Pre-register the direction (negative
slope for early-stopping models, null for fingerprint models) before fitting.

**Cost.** Minutes. Two committed tables and a join.

**Expected benefit.** Either a fourth mechanism for the project's failure catalogue — a specific,
easily-missed protocol defect that inflates deep-model variance and would make any such comparison
unreliable — or a clean null that removes a plausible confound from the existing comparison. Both
are worth having. The first would also be actionable advice: stratify validation folds by class,
which costs nothing.

**Validation criteria.** 20 seeds is a small sample for a slope; report the CI and treat
p > 0.05 as a null rather than a trend. The control group must show no effect, or the finding is
about something else. If the result is positive it must replicate on CDK2 and MAO-B before entering
the paper, matching the standard the project already holds itself to.

**Risk.** Medium-high as a *finding* — 20 points, one target, and a post-hoc hypothesis generated
from the same data that would test it. The honest framing is that experiment 3 is exploratory and
needs the CDK2/MAO-B replication to count. It is cheap enough to be worth the look regardless.

### Not recommended now

- **Any new model family.** Ten entrants are already run and the ceiling is a fingerprint random
  forest. Adding an eleventh does not address the supervisor's objection.
- **An LLM atomic-reasoning curation pipeline**, yet. It is a genuine idea (§2) but it improves data
  curation, not prediction, and it needs a manually verified sample to evaluate against. Build the
  sample first — 30–50 molecule-measurement-source triples checked by hand — and the experiment
  becomes well-posed. That sample is also useful on its own.
- **A third replication target.** The CDK2/MAO-B sign disagreement is real but it is a bound on one
  secondary claim, and `professor_summary.md` already asks the supervisor whether it needs
  resolving. Wait for that answer rather than spending a target on it.

## 6. Novelty framing, unchanged but sharpened

`professor_summary.md` §5 assesses the methodological novelty as nil and that assessment stands. The
contribution is empirical: the publication as the grouping unit, the non-identifiability of assay
and publication in ChEMBL, the decomposition showing ~92% of CDK2's protocol gap is the aggregation
unit rather than generalisation, and the design constraint that a leave-one-publication-out protocol
cannot support a guaranteed hierarchical calibration at any error rate.

Experiment 1 could add a fifth item of the same kind: that the protocol gap is *asymmetric across
classes*, which would mean the field's threshold-free reporting of it hides which direction the
model fails in. That is the sort of specific, checkable observation this project is good at, and it
fits the existing framing rather than competing with it.
