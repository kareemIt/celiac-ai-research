# Experiment 2: activity cliffs on TG2, CDK2 and MAO-B (run 2026-11-09)

> **Followed up later the same day — see [`validation_2026-11-09.md`](validation_2026-11-09.md) §3.**
> The missing SMILES-Levenshtein similarity test has since been implemented in-repo, without adding a
> dependency. It is **not material**: cliff counts rise 0.6–3.3%, the median change in cliff ranking
> accuracy is 0.005, and cliffs are harder in 58 of 58 comparisons rather than 57 of 58. Two TG2
> Holm significances are lost. The numbers below are the two-test definition and remain the record
> of the first run; `results/cliff_ranking_*.csv` now carries both definitions side by side.

Reproduce: `python scripts/analyse_cliffs.py`.
Outputs: `results/cliff_pairs_<target>.csv` (the pair inventory),
`results/cliff_ranking_<target>.csv` (per model × arm × fold × pair kind),
`results/cliff_ranking_<target>_summary.csv`, `results/cliff_ranking_<target>_tests.csv`.
Tests: `tests/test_cliffs.py`. Cost: 31 seconds of CPU, no GPU, no retraining.
Plan and pre-registration: [`review_2026-11-09.md`](review_2026-11-09.md) §5, experiment 2.

**The gap this closes.** `state_of_the_art_comparison.md` §1.0 calls van Tilborg, Alenicheva &
Grisoni (*JCIM* 2022;62(23):5938–5951, doi:10.1021/acs.jcim.2c01073) "the most relevant paper to
our situation" and no cliff analysis had ever been run on our data. The similarity-band work in
`generalization.md` bins molecules by *distance to training*; a cliff is a pair of *mutually
similar* molecules with divergent potency, which is a different measurement.

**Two answers.** Activity cliffs are harder for every model on every target — **57 of 58 model × arm
comparisons, 18 of them significant after Holm**. But the field's framing that overall performance
*fails to predict* cliff performance does not hold inside our scaffold arms, where the two rank
models the same way (ρ 0.76–0.89). It only breaks down under publication shift.

---

## 1. Definition, taken from the source rather than invented

MoleculeACE's published parameters, read off `MoleculeACE/benchmark/cliffs.py` and the repository's
own usage example (`similarity=0.9`, `potency_fold=10`):

> A pair is an activity cliff when it is **structurally similar by at least one** test — Tanimoto
> ≥ 0.9 on Morgan fingerprints (radius 2, 1024 bits), or Tanimoto ≥ 0.9 on Morgan fingerprints of
> the **generic Murcko scaffold** — **and** its potency differs by more than **10-fold**
> (|ΔpIC50| > 1).

Note the project's own `chem.morgan` uses 2048 bits; 1024 is used here because that is what the
cliff definition specifies. Potency comes from the same loaders that produced the labels
(`tg2.combined("regression")`, `chembl.regression`), so SMILES join to the committed splits exactly.

**Deviation, recorded.** MoleculeACE ORs in a third test, normalised SMILES Levenshtein similarity
≥ 0.9. No edit-distance library is installed and adding one would alter the pinned environment every
committed result was produced under. Because the tests are OR-ed, dropping one can only **shrink**
the cliff set: every pair called a cliff here is a cliff by MoleculeACE's rule, and some cliffs are
missed. A SMILES-string similarity would in any case not be comparable across sources, since our
canonicalisation keeps the largest fragment and theirs does not.

**Scoring is pairwise, which removes the threshold question.** For a discordant pair — one potent,
one not — whose two molecules land in the same test fold, the model is right if it gives the potent
one the higher probability; ties score 0.5. No operating point is involved, so none of the
threshold machinery in [`per_class_2026-11-09.md`](per_class_2026-11-09.md) is needed.

**Three kinds of pair, enumerated inside each fold.** The comparison needs a control at a *matched
potency gap*, or "cliffs are hard" cannot be distinguished from "big potency gaps are hard":

| kind | similar? | fold change | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cliff` | yes | > 10 | the MoleculeACE case |
| `distant` | no | > 10 | **the control**: same potency gap, different chemistry |
| `near_threshold` | yes | ≤ 10 | both molecules straddle the 1 µM cut by less than 10×, so the labels are nearly arbitrary |

## 2. The pair inventory

| target | similar discordant pairs | cliffs | near-threshold | undecidable (censored, no pIC50) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TG2 | 1,840 | **705** | 765 | 370 (20%) |
| CDK2 | 9,406 | **5,210** | 1,794 | 2,402 (26%) |
| MAO-B | 72,054 | **51,124** | 12,218 | 8,712 (12%) |

All three clear the 100-cliff bar set in advance by a wide margin. The undecidable column is the
cost of the project's censored negatives: a molecule recorded only as "> 10 µM" has no pIC50, so
MoleculeACE's potency test cannot be applied to it even though its *label* is sound. Those pairs are
excluded from everything below rather than guessed at.

Per-fold counts matter more than totals, because a fold is the unit of the test, and they are thin
on TG2: a median of 26 cliff pairs per scaffold fold and **5.5 per `scaffold_pub` fold**, against 96
and 143 on CDK2 and 732 to 1,166 on MAO-B. TG2 per-fold accuracies are therefore coarse-grained, and
TG2 results here should be read as the weakest of the three.

## 3. Cliffs are harder, on all three targets

Pairwise ranking accuracy, mean over folds, `scaffold_pub` (the arm all three targets share):

| target | model | cliff | distant | Δ | Holm p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CDK2 | ChemBERTa-2 frozen | 0.635 | 0.841 | **−0.206** | 0.006 |
| CDK2 | ChemBERTa-2 fine-tuned | 0.677 | 0.870 | −0.193 | 0.016 |
| CDK2 | CheMeleon | 0.742 | 0.909 | −0.168 | 0.021 |
| CDK2 | Random forest | 0.761 | 0.901 | −0.140 | 0.021 |
| CDK2 | XGBoost | 0.765 | 0.901 | −0.136 | 0.003 |
| TG2 | Chemprop | 0.490 | 0.815 | **−0.325** | 0.008 |
| TG2 | Chemprop + RDKit 2D | 0.514 | 0.839 | −0.325 | 0.010 |
| TG2 | Random forest | 0.777 | 0.900 | −0.123 | 0.126 |
| MAO-B | Logistic regression | 0.728 | 0.824 | −0.097 | 0.010 |
| MAO-B | Random forest | 0.768 | 0.842 | −0.074 | 0.010 |
| MAO-B | CheMeleon | 0.790 | 0.848 | −0.058 | 0.021 |

Across every target and arm: **cliff accuracy is below distant accuracy in 57 of 58 comparisons**
(TG2 29/30, CDK2 15/15, MAO-B 13/13), 18 significant after Holm within each arm. The single
exception is ChemBERTa-2 frozen on TG2 `paper_lopo`, +0.014 on eight folds of fifteen pairs — noise.
Tests are paired per fold, because one molecule sits in many pairs and pairs are not independent,
while each fold contributes a single number.

**On TG2 `scaffold_pub`, Chemprop ranks cliff pairs at 0.490 — below chance.** It is not merely
weaker on cliffs; on this arm it inverts them.

This replicates van Tilborg et al.'s central claim on three targets, with our ten entrants, against
a control the original design does not have: the same potency gap at low structural similarity. The
drop is not an artifact of large potency differences being hard — those are the `distant` pairs, and
they are the *easy* ones.

## 4. Where the published framing does not replicate

The paper's headline framing is that overall accuracy does not predict cliff accuracy. Correlating
the two across models, one Spearman per arm:

| target | arm | ρ over models | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| MAO-B | `scaffold_pub` | **0.886** | 0.019 |
| TG2 | `scaffold_pub` | **0.770** | 0.009 |
| TG2 | `scaffold` | **0.758** | 0.011 |
| CDK2 | `scaffold_pub` | 0.657 | 0.156 |
| TG2 | `paper_lopo` | 0.515 | 0.128 |
| MAO-B | `paper_grouped` | 0.429 | 0.397 |
| CDK2 | `paper_grouped` | 0.143 | 0.787 |

Inside the scaffold arms, the ranking on ordinary pairs **does** predict the ranking on cliffs,
positively and significantly on three of four. Under publication shift the correlation falls to
0.14–0.43 and none is significant. So the right statement for our data is narrower than the
published one: *cliffs cost every model accuracy, but choosing a model by overall score is not
misleading within-distribution — it becomes misleading under shift.*

Two reasons to hold this loosely. Each correlation is over 6 to 10 models, so the confidence
intervals are wide and the apparent ordering of the seven rows should not be read as a trend. And
the shifted arms have both fewer models and fewer folds, so lower power alone could produce the
pattern. This is reported as a qualification on a cited paper, not as a refutation of it.

Individual models still reorder even where the aggregate correlation holds. On TG2 `scaffold`
CheMeleon is sixth of ten on distant pairs (0.830) and **first on cliffs** (0.799, the smallest drop
of any entrant at −0.030), while XGBoost goes from second (0.870) to sixth (0.715). Picking a model
for a cliff-rich series by its headline AUROC would pick the wrong one.

## 5. An incidental finding about the binary task

`near_threshold` pairs — structurally similar molecules whose potencies differ by less than 10× but
which fall on opposite sides of the 1 µM cut — are ranked at 0.41 to 0.73, mostly between 0.50 and
0.65. Models are close to chance on them, which is the correct behaviour: the label difference
inside such a pair is largely an artifact of where the threshold was drawn, not a chemical fact.

There are **765 such pairs on TG2, 1,794 on CDK2 and 12,218 on MAO-B**. They are a floor on binary
classification accuracy that no model can raise and that no threshold-free metric identifies. This
strengthens a point the project already makes about regression versus classification on potency
data, and it is cheap to state: a share of every reported classification error on these datasets is
unlearnable by construction.

## 6. What this adds, and what it does not

**Adds.** Gap 3 from the review is closed. A replication of the activity-cliff penalty on three
targets with a matched-potency-gap control, a partial non-replication of the paper's
performance-does-not-predict-cliffs framing with the condition under which it *does* hold, a
below-chance case (Chemprop on TG2 `scaffold_pub`), and a count of pairs the binary task cannot
learn.

**Does not add.** No new model and no improvement to any model. Nothing about TG2 inhibition or
celiac disease. No causal claim about why cliffs are hard.

**Not done, and limits.** The Levenshtein similarity test is missing (§1), so the cliff set is a
subset of MoleculeACE's. Censored negatives are excluded entirely, which drops 12–26% of similar
discordant pairs and does so non-randomly — they are the weakest-potency molecules. TG2's per-fold
pair counts are small enough that its arms carry much less weight than CDK2's and MAO-B's. The
ranking correlations in §4 rest on 6–10 models each. And no sensitivity sweep over the 0.9 / 10×
parameters was run: they were fixed to the published values first, as pre-registered, and a sweep
would be a separate, explicitly secondary analysis.
