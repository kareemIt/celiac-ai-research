## 2. Statistical units and multiple comparisons

The audit question was whether every test uses the correct unit of independence. One real defect:

**§4.5 previously declared "molecules are the independent unit" globally.** That is true for
scaffold and random splitting and false for every publication-held-out comparison, where molecules
from one paper form a correlated block — one congeneric series, one assay, one laboratory. §4.5 now
states the unit per regime and records that §5.14 and §5.15 use a cluster bootstrap over
publications. The underlying analyses were already correct (`PaperBootstrap` in
`scripts/validate_novelty_share.py` and the bootstrap in `scripts/analyse_protocol_gap.py` both
resample publications); the methods section misdescribed them.

Checks performed, all passing:

- **Pairing.** §5.14 pairs across publications, which is legitimate because a publication is the
  same molecule set in both arms. The split-penalty arms are *not* paired — 10–20 scaffold seeds
  against 10–11 publication folds, with no seed-to-fold correspondence — and correctly use Welch's
  unpaired t-test. §5.13 pairs per fold *and* model, where both regimes score identical molecules.
- **Corrections.** Holm is applied and deferred to within each model family. This is what leaves
  §5.11's ranking inversion inconclusive (every Holm p = 1.000), §5.10's best entrant significant
  (Holm p = 0.045), no MAO-B per-model penalty significant, and CheMeleon's three-target advantage
  at Holm p = 0.057 — reported as exploratory rather than as a finding.
- **Pooling across targets.** Inverse-variance fixed-effect with Cochran's Q reported. CheMeleon's
  publication-arm pooling is homogeneous (Q = 0.09, df = 2); its scaffold-arm counterpart is not
  (Q = 9.71) and is not claimed.
- **Undefined folds.** Single-class leave-one-publication-out folds give undefined AUROC (20 of 83
  on CDK2). They are dropped *and counted*, because dropping is itself a selection effect.

No test was found using the wrong unit, and no uncorrected family comparison is reported as
significant.

## 3. Does publication-wise conformal calibration have a formal guarantee?

**No, and the paper now says so.** Split conformal attains 1 − α coverage when calibration and test
points are exchangeable. Under our regime they are not:

1. A calibration molecule and a test molecule come from different publications, and molecules within
   a publication are strongly dependent. The exchangeability that fails for i.i.d. calibration fails
   here too.
2. A guarantee for an observation from a **previously unseen group** requires hierarchical
   exchangeability plus an estimator built for it — hierarchical conformal prediction [21, 22],
   which uses subsampling or a quantile-of-quantiles. We run plain split conformal with a grouped
   calibration set, which is a different procedure and inherits no theorem.
3. **Group exchangeability is violated by our own design.** `group_calibration` fills the
   calibration set with whole publications *smallest-first*, to keep the fitting set large.
   Measured consequence: calibration publications average 11.9 molecules on both replication
   targets against a mean test-publication size near 26, and 19.6 against a 38-molecule median on
   TG2 — the calibration groups are about half the size of the group being predicted.

What grouped calibration does is make the calibration distribution resemble the test distribution,
shrinking the coverage gap in the sense of Barber et al. [23] without closing it. The result is
reported as **measured coverage on three targets** with no distribution-free claim. §9 names the
experiment that would settle it, which needs no new model fits.

**Marginal coverage is also distinguished throughout from a decisive answer.** Coverage is restored
on all three targets; the singleton fraction is 0.15–0.32 (TG2), 0.41–0.48 (CDK2) and 0.46–0.56
(MAO-B), and errors among singletons are 0.16–0.39. Reporting coverage alone would have hidden this
everywhere.

## 4. Is the calibration result novel?

**The method is not. The measurement is.** Searching the conformal and cheminformatics literature:

- Conformal prediction in drug discovery, including as an applicability-domain method and in
  Mondrian form for imbalanced bioactivity data, is well established [24, 25].
- Group-aware calibration is established: Mondrian CP conditions on a taxonomy [6, 24], clustered
  conformal prediction calibrates per cluster of groups [26], and hierarchical CP handles the
  two-level sampling structure with a guarantee for unseen groups [21, 22].
- Barber et al. [23] give the general account of coverage loss under broken exchangeability.
- Scaffold-split optimism is an active topic [9, 10, 27].

So any claim of a *new method* would be wrong, and the paper now states this in §2.3. What we did
not find is a report of **publication-held-out conformal coverage on a bioactivity dataset** — a
grouping that is not optional in literature-derived data and that in ChEMBL coincides exactly with
the assay. That is the contribution, and it is empirical and narrow.

**Missing-citation risk remains.** The search was keyword-driven over four queries and did not
include a systematic scan of the cheminformatics conformal literature, where Norinder, Spjuth,
Svensson and co-workers have a large output. A reviewer may well know a paper that reports exactly
this. References 24 and 25 also need verifying against publisher records.

## 6. Addendum, same day: the hierarchical conformal comparison (§5.16)

Blocking item 2 above is closed. `scripts/run_hcp.py` implements split hierarchical conformal
prediction (Theorem 1 of Lee, Barber & Willett, arXiv:2306.06342), `tests/test_hcp.py` verifies it
against its own theorem, `scripts/analyse_hcp.py` assembles the comparison. No model was refitted.

### Integrity of the inputs, checked first

Saved publication-arm predictions for all three targets: **0** duplicate
(fold, regime, model, alpha, molecule) rows, **0** molecules tested in more than one fold, **0**
label disagreements against the splits files, **0** molecules without a publication id. Molecule
counts per model are exactly 444 / 2,078 / 5,091. Original outputs were read, never written.

### What the saved files could not support

One required input is genuinely missing, and it bounds the experiment. §5.13's i.i.d. arm
calibrates on random molecules drawn from the *training* publications, so reconstructing it needs
nonconformity scores for molecules the model was fitted on. Only out-of-fold predictions were
saved. Rather than refit, §5.16 compares the two group-aware methods — which is the comparison the
experiment was commissioned for — and reports a milder molecule-pooled surrogate in place of the
i.i.d. arm, labelled as such. §5.13's measurement of the i.i.d. arm stands on its own run.

### Feasibility is itself a finding

HCP needs K₁ ≥ ⌈1/α⌉ − 1 calibration groups or its quantile lands on the infinite atom:

| target | publications per fold | α = 0.1 (needs 9) | α = 0.2 (needs 4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| TG2 | 1 | infeasible | infeasible |
| CDK2 | 6–9 | infeasible | all 10 folds |
| MAO-B | 17–22 | all 10 folds | all 10 folds |

**Leave-one-publication-out cannot support a guaranteed calibration at any α.** The protocol behind
our headline result is the one protocol that cannot justify it. This is reported in §5.16 and
drives §10.2's conditional recommendation.

### Results

HCP holds its nominal floor in **54 of 54** runs (3 targets × 2 designs where feasible × 6 models ×
2 α). Our publication-grouped method holds it in **22 of 54**, and in the theorem-faithful
within-fold design in **5 of 18**, with 2 runs whose entire publication-clustered interval lies
below target. Paired on publication, HCP − ours is positive in all 54 comparisons and the interval
excludes zero in 52.

The implementation check that matters: in the within-fold design, where Theorem 1 applies, no run's
interval lies above the theorem's own upper bound 1 − α + 2/(K₁+1), and over a K₁ sweep from 9 to
187 the measured excess coverage decays onto that bound (+0.095 → +0.011 against 0.200 → 0.011).

Cost: at α = 0.1 on MAO-B within-fold the singleton fraction falls 0.615 → 0.308. HCP halves the
decisive answers to buy its floor, and the trade nearly vanishes at large K₁ (0.560 → 0.529).

### 7.2 The novelty claim was too strong

**Correction 18.** §2.3 previously ended: *"What we have not found in the cheminformatics literature
is a report of publication-held-out conformal coverage on a bioactivity dataset, which is the gap we
fill."* The systematic scan found **Rakhshaninejad, Jurgens, Dewolf & Waegeman, arXiv:2505.18890
(2025)**, now reference 28: conformal prediction for drug–target interaction on KIBA under four
splits including drug-based, protein-based and fully unseen drug–protein pairs, comparing marginal,
group-conditioned and three cluster-conditioned variants and reporting subgroup coverage. That asks
our question on a different grouping, and it is close.

§2.3 has been rewritten to claim only what survives: the methodological novelty is **nil**; the
empirical contribution is (i) the *source publication* as the grouping, which in ChEMBL coincides
exactly with the assay so that the two are not separately identifiable, (ii) a comparison that
includes a method with a finite-sample guarantee for an unseen group, which [28] does not test, and
(iii) the design constraint that leave-one-publication-out cannot support a guaranteed calibration
at α = 0.1. Item (iii) is the most practically useful and we have not seen it stated.

### 7.3 The exchangeability justification was wrong — and the correct one is stronger

**Correction 19.** §5.16 and `scripts/run_hcp.py` justified the within-fold design by saying "our
grouped K-fold assigns publications to folds at random". Reading `deal_papers`
(`scripts/audit_replication_feasibility.py:69`) shows it does not: publications are dealt
**largest-first to the lightest fold** to equalise fold molecule counts. A fold's publications are
a size-balanced selection, not a random sample.

The design is nevertheless theorem-compatible, for a better reason. Theorem 1 needs only the K₁
calibration groups and the test group to be exchangeable, and `pick_calibration` partitions the
fold's publications **uniformly at random**. Conditional on the fold's publication set, that
randomisation delivers exchangeability by construction, whatever the corpus looks like — an
assumption imposed rather than hoped for. Two limits now stated in the paper: the guarantee is
conditional on that set, and because fold sizes are balanced the within-fold publication-size mix is
not the corpus mix, so these figures are not a claim about a publication drawn at random from the
literature; and the smallest-first and largest-first runs remove the randomisation deliberately, so
the theorem does not cover them and they are a sensitivity analysis only.

### 7.4 Items re-checked and found sound

- **Model fitting.** For fold k the scoring model was fitted on the other folds minus the
  smallest-first calibration publications of the *training* set. Both the calibration and the test
  publications of the within-fold design lie in fold k, so the score function is independent of
  them, as Theorem 1 requires.
- **Theoretical versus empirical coverage.** The paper states the floor as a theorem for HCP and
  reports every number as measured. No coverage figure is presented as guaranteed, and §5.13's
  method is labelled empirical throughout.
- **Publication-clustered uncertainty and model dependence.** Every interval in §5.14–§5.16 is a
  cluster bootstrap over publications; §4.5 states the unit per regime. §5.16 says in text that the
  six entrants share folds and molecules, so its 54 paired comparisons are 54 per-model statements,
  not 54 independent observations.
- **Matched-publication comparisons.** `check_paper.py` asserts, as a hard assertion rather than a
  reported statistic, that the `same_molecules` rows have identical molecule counts in both arms.
- **Novelty common support.** §5.14 and §8 both state the share is an association, that the residual
  crosses zero for every model on both targets, and that it is not estimable on TG2 for want of
  common support.
- **Singletons versus both-label sets.** Reported side by side with coverage in §5.13, §5.16 and
  §10.2, and §10.2 requires both to be reported together.
