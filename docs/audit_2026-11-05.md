## 2. Statistical units and multiple comparisons

The audit question was whether every test uses the correct unit of independence. One real defect:

**§4.5 previously declared "molecules are the independent unit" globally.** That is true for
scaffold and random splitting and false for every publication-held-out comparison, where molecules
from one paper form a correlated block — one congeneric series, one assay, one laboratory. §4.5 now
states the unit per regime and records that §5.14 and §5.15 use a cluster bootstrap over
publications. The underlying analyses were already correct (`PaperBootstrap` in
`scripts/validate_novelty_share.py` and the bootstrap in `scripts/analyse_protocol_gap.py` both
resample publications); the methods section misdescribed them.

Checks performed, all passing:

- **Pairing.** §5.14 pairs across publications, which is legitimate because a publication is the
  same molecule set in both arms. The split-penalty arms are *not* paired — 10–20 scaffold seeds
  against 10–11 publication folds, with no seed-to-fold correspondence — and correctly use Welch's
  unpaired t-test. §5.13 pairs per fold *and* model, where both regimes score identical molecules.
- **Corrections.** Holm is applied and deferred to within each model family. This is what leaves
  §5.11's ranking inversion inconclusive (every Holm p = 1.000), §5.10's best entrant significant
  (Holm p = 0.045), no MAO-B per-model penalty significant, and CheMeleon's three-target advantage
  at Holm p = 0.057 — reported as exploratory rather than as a finding.
- **Pooling across targets.** Inverse-variance fixed-effect with Cochran's Q reported. CheMeleon's
  publication-arm pooling is homogeneous (Q = 0.09, df = 2); its scaffold-arm counterpart is not
  (Q = 9.71) and is not claimed.
- **Undefined folds.** Single-class leave-one-publication-out folds give undefined AUROC (20 of 83
  on CDK2). They are dropped *and counted*, because dropping is itself a selection effect.

No test was found using the wrong unit, and no uncorrected family comparison is reported as
significant.

## 3. Does publication-wise conformal calibration have a formal guarantee?

**No, and the paper now says so.** Split conformal attains 1 − α coverage when calibration and test
points are exchangeable. Under our regime they are not:

1. A calibration molecule and a test molecule come from different publications, and molecules within
   a publication are strongly dependent. The exchangeability that fails for i.i.d. calibration fails
   here too.
2. A guarantee for an observation from a **previously unseen group** requires hierarchical
   exchangeability plus an estimator built for it — hierarchical conformal prediction [21, 22],
   which uses subsampling or a quantile-of-quantiles. We run plain split conformal with a grouped
   calibration set, which is a different procedure and inherits no theorem.
3. **Group exchangeability is violated by our own design.** `group_calibration` fills the
   calibration set with whole publications *smallest-first*, to keep the fitting set large.
   Measured consequence: calibration publications average 11.9 molecules on both replication
   targets against a mean test-publication size near 26, and 19.6 against a 38-molecule median on
   TG2 — the calibration groups are about half the size of the group being predicted.

What grouped calibration does is make the calibration distribution resemble the test distribution,
shrinking the coverage gap in the sense of Barber et al. [23] without closing it. The result is
reported as **measured coverage on three targets** with no distribution-free claim. §9 names the
experiment that would settle it, which needs no new model fits.

**Marginal coverage is also distinguished throughout from a decisive answer.** Coverage is restored
on all three targets; the singleton fraction is 0.15–0.32 (TG2), 0.41–0.48 (CDK2) and 0.46–0.56
(MAO-B), and errors among singletons are 0.16–0.39. Reporting coverage alone would have hidden this
everywhere.

## 4. Is the calibration result novel?

**The method is not. The measurement is.** Searching the conformal and cheminformatics literature:

- Conformal prediction in drug discovery, including as an applicability-domain method and in
  Mondrian form for imbalanced bioactivity data, is well established [24, 25].
- Group-aware calibration is established: Mondrian CP conditions on a taxonomy [6, 24], clustered
  conformal prediction calibrates per cluster of groups [26], and hierarchical CP handles the
  two-level sampling structure with a guarantee for unseen groups [21, 22].
- Barber et al. [23] give the general account of coverage loss under broken exchangeability.
- Scaffold-split optimism is an active topic [9, 10, 27].

So any claim of a *new method* would be wrong, and the paper now states this in §2.3. What we did
not find is a report of **publication-held-out conformal coverage on a bioactivity dataset** — a
grouping that is not optional in literature-derived data and that in ChEMBL coincides exactly with
the assay. That is the contribution, and it is empirical and narrow.

**Missing-citation risk remains.** The search was keyword-driven over four queries and did not
include a systematic scan of the cheminformatics conformal literature, where Norinder, Spjuth,
Svensson and co-workers have a large output. A reviewer may well know a paper that reports exactly
this. References 24 and 25 also need verifying against publisher records.

## 5. Publication readiness

**Ready as a methods contribution; not ready to submit.**

Supported well enough to publish: the conformal coverage failure and its empirical grouped fix
(three targets, two protocols, every p < 10⁻⁷); the aggregation-unit artefact (about 92% of CDK2's
macro gap, with the grouped arm's own predictions re-aggregated as the control); the
assay/publication non-identifiability (0 of 380 and 0 of 516); and the four original TG2 failure
modes with measured before-and-after effects. §5.14 being a correction to our own earlier claim
strengthens rather than weakens this, provided it is presented as such.

Must not be claimed: that publication identity causes the residual decline; that the novelty share
is a causal decomposition; that grouped conformal calibration is guaranteed; that any penalty
magnitude transfers to another dataset.

Blocking items before submission, in priority order:

1. **Verify references 24 and 25**, and run one systematic literature scan for prior reports of
   publication- or assay-held-out conformal coverage in cheminformatics. Cheap, and it protects the
   only novelty claim in the paper.
2. **Run hierarchical and clustered conformal prediction on the saved folds** (§9). No new model
   fits. Converts the headline result from an empirical regularity into a method comparison, and
   tests whether the smallest-first grouping is costing coverage.
3. **Choose a venue and compress.** The six-failure structure suits a methods or cheminformatics
   journal; at current length it suits no conference.

Not blocking, and worth stating as limitations rather than closing:

4. A third protocol-matched target would settle §5.15's sign disagreement. Real scientific return,
   but the paper is coherent without it because the disagreement is reported as a constraint.
5. Chemprop on CDK2 and MAO-B would separate pretraining from architecture for CheMeleon. Needed
   only if CheMeleon becomes a claim; it is currently exploratory.
6. The assay confound cannot be closed within ChEMBL at all. It is a limitation, not a gap.

**Largest remaining methodological weakness:** the headline result's theoretical standing. We report
an empirical fix to a guarantee failure, using a grouping that violates the assumption the guarantee
would need, without comparing against the published method that has the theorem. Item 2 addresses it
at low cost and should be done before submission rather than after review.
